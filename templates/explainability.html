How Does Grad-CAM Explain Diabetic Retinopathy?

Standard deep neural networks are often criticized as "black boxes" because they output a probability score without showing how they arrived at that decision. In rural healthcare, where healthcare workers and general practitioners triage patients without an on-site ophthalmologist, transparent explainability is mandatory for clinical trust and patient safety.

Target Convolutional Layer

In our ResNet-50 architecture, Grad-CAM targets the final residual block (layer4[-1]), where spatial resolution is preserved alongside high-level semantic retinal features.

Gradient Backpropagation

Gradients of the predicted severity score \(Y^c\) are computed with respect to feature maps \(A^k\). Global average pooling calculates importance weights \(\alpha_k^c\) for each visual channel.

Weighted Activation Mapping

A linear combination of feature maps weighted by \(\alpha_k^c\) is passed through a ReLU activation to retain features that positively support the predicted diabetic retinopathy grade.

Thermal Colormap Overlay

The normalized saliency map is converted using a thermal colormap (JET) and alpha-blended over the fundus image: Red areas denote critical model focus; Blue areas denote low interest.

Clinical Interpretation Guidelines
  • Neural Attention ≠ Histological Confirmation: Highlighted red regions represent areas that influenced the deep network's feature activations, not guaranteed lesion pathology.
  • Check for Flash Artifacts: Central corneal flash reflections or uneven illumination can occasionally attract spurious attention. The automated quality guard helps filter these out.
  • Anatomical Correlation: Look whether red heatmaps overlap with visible microaneurysms, blot hemorrhages, or exudate clusters.
  • Always Refer High Grades: Regardless of heatmap appearance, any patient flagged with Grade 2 or higher requires referral to a district ophthalmology centre.
